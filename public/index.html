Know your local candidates.

Did you know?

School boards and many local races do not list party labels on the ballot.

Which races use nonpartisan ballots?

School boards, township boards, and city and village races covered here use nonpartisan ballots. So do the Nebraska Legislature, State Board of Education, public power district, and educational service unit races in this guide.

Why it matters

These officials help shape policies and public spending, and party affiliation is one consideration alongside a candidate's experience and stated positions.

About this guide

How to read this directory

The Gage County GOP publishes this directory of selected 2026 candidate races and judicial retention questions in Gage County. The directory does not include every contest on your ballot.

Inclusion and endorsements

The Gage County GOP applies consistent sourcing and presentation standards to comparable entries. Inclusion of a candidate or judge does not indicate a Gage County GOP endorsement.

How information is verified

Each candidate card cites the source for its displayed claims, and different sources establish different facts.

Party registration
Voter-registration affiliations are checked through Nebraska VoterCheck or the Gage County Voter Roll. Each entry identifies its source.
Primary participation
A named partisan primary in the Gage County Electionware report confirms participation in that party's primary contest. It does not confirm voter registration.
Current filings
The county filing list identifies regular filings, petition candidacies, and declared write-ins. A write-in declaration does not mean the name is printed on the ballot. State and federal races use the final statewide general-election candidate list.
Election results
Gage County vote totals come from the county Electionware report, which is labeled UNOFFICIAL RESULTS. The report covers Gage County reporting only, and the official state canvass establishes statewide and multi-county primary nominees.
Election stage
The directory separates current listings from primary history. Primary history includes candidates who did not advance, candidates who withdrew, and primary nominees absent from the final general-election list.
Pending verification
The directory uses "Affiliation not confirmed" when no affiliation source is confirmed. Reviewers do not infer an affiliation.
Reported affiliation
Some affiliations are reported by Voter Information Project. Those entries identify the voter guide as their source and are distinct from direct voter-registration checks.

Ballot and affiliation information

What "nonpartisan" means here

In a nonpartisan race, party labels do not appear beside candidate names on the ballot. A candidate may still have a publicly documented political affiliation.

Voter-registration affiliation, ballot party, and participation in a party primary are different facts. Each affiliation label identifies the evidence that supports it.

County, state, and federal sections also include partisan races. Consult official election materials for the contests on your ballot.

Frequently asked questions

Questions about the directory

Are all races in this directory nonpartisan?

No. Party labels do not appear beside candidate names in nonpartisan races, and the directory also includes partisan county, state, and federal races.

Does a listed affiliation mean the candidate is officially running as a party nominee?

Not necessarily. The directory includes races with different ballot rules. A publicly documented political affiliation does not establish nomination status, so consult official election materials for the specific race.

How was this information verified?

Registered affiliation, ballot party, primary participation, and election results are supported by different sources.

Why are some candidates listed under primary history?

Candidates in primary history participated in the primary and are not currently listed for the general election. Each card identifies non-advancement, withdrawal, or absence from the final list when a source supports the status.

What happens if an affiliation changes?

An entry should be updated when a reliable public source documents an affiliation change. Each card shows when its source was reviewed.

Does this site endorse candidates?

Inclusion in this directory does not indicate a Gage County GOP endorsement. Candidates receive the same sourcing and presentation standards.

How can an error be corrected?

Send a supporting public source to the correction contact listed under "Sources, review, and corrections".

How do judicial retention votes work?

Nebraska judges are appointed by the governor through a merit selection process. In a retention election, voters decide separately whether each judge should remain in office for another six-year term. For each judge, vote Yes to retain or No against retention.
